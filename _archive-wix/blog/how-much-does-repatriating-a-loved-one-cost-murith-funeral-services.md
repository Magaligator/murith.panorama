## What Is Body Repatriation?

Body repatriation, also known as international funeral transportation or the repatriation of human remains, refers to the process of transporting a deceased person from the place of death to the country or city where they will be buried or cremated.

In Switzerland, this process is subject to strict regulations and involves several parties, including funeral directors, cantonal authorities, consulates and transport companies.

The term “repatriation” is commonly used when the deceased is a foreign national and the family wishes to arrange the funeral in their country of origin. Other terms you may come across include:

- International funeral transportation: a general term for transporting a deceased person between two countries.
- Funeral cortege: the transportation of the coffin by road or air.
- Repatriation of human remains: a term commonly used in consular procedures.
- Return of the deceased: a more general expression used by some families and organisations.
- Transportation or transfer of human remains: administrative terms that may be used in official and legal documents.

## What Factors Influence the Cost of Body Repatriation?

Several factors determine the final cost of international funeral transportation from Switzerland. Understanding these factors can help you anticipate the expenses involved and make the decisions that are most appropriate for your situation.


### 1. The Method of Transportation

Air transportation is the most common option for long-distance repatriation, but it involves specific requirements, including the use of a coffin approved for air transport.

Road transportation may be possible for nearby European destinations and can offer families greater flexibility. Where appropriate, a family member may also be able to accompany their loved one in the funeral vehicle.


#### Comparison of Funeral Transportation Methods

| Criteria | Air Transportation | Road Transportation |
| Distance covered | Worldwide | Mainly Europe |
| Transportation cost only | CHF 1,500–5,000 | CHF 800–3,000 |
| Average timeframe | 2–7 days | 1–5 days |
| Family accompaniment | Possible (separate passenger seat) | Yes, in the funeral vehicle |
| Coffin requirements | IATA-approved coffin required | Standard requirements |

### 2. Preservation Care and Embalming

When arranging the repatriation of a deceased person, preservation care, also known as thanatopraxy or embalming, is often required, particularly for air transportation. These procedures involve hygienic and preservation treatments designed to maintain the deceased in the best possible condition throughout the journey to their final destination.

The cost generally ranges from CHF 800 to CHF 2,500, depending on the procedures and services required.


### 3. Coffin and Funeral Equipment

The type of coffin used for international repatriation must comply with specific regulations. Airlines may require an approved coffin, often consisting of a sealed zinc inner container enclosed within a second wooden coffin or outer casing.

Murith Care Everywhere offers a wide range of coffins suitable for different regulatory, cultural and religious requirements.


### 4. Administrative Formalities

Repatriating a deceased person from Switzerland involves a number of mandatory administrative procedures. Murith Funeral Services handles these formalities in coordination with the relevant authorities, consulates and other organisations involved in the process.

The procedures may include:

- Obtaining the death certificate from the Geneva civil registry office.
- Contacting the consulate or embassy of the destination country.
- Obtaining the required laissez-passer for the international transportation of human remains.
- Coordinating with the relevant cantonal health authorities.
- Arranging for the deceased to be received by a local funeral service at the destination.
- Organising the transportation, whether by air or by road.

## Essential Documents Required for International Body Repatriation

Preparing the administrative file is a crucial step in the body repatriation process. Any delay in providing the required documents may result in the transportation being postponed. Murith Funeral Services coordinates these procedures by liaising with the relevant authorities, consulates and embassies.

The documents generally required for the repatriation of a deceased person from Switzerland may include:

- The official death certificate issued by the Geneva or relevant cantonal civil registry office
- The medical death certificate stating the cause of death
- The international mortuary passport (laissez-passer mortuaire) or burial permit
- The deceased’s passport or identity card
- The burial authorisation issued by the authorities in the destination country
- The certificate confirming that the deceased has been placed in a hermetically sealed coffin, signed by the relevant doctor
- A certificate confirming preservation or embalming care, where applicable
- Transportation documents, such as an air waybill or road transportation documents

## Burial and Funeral Arrangements: The Available Options

When a deceased person is repatriated to their country of origin, the funeral and burial arrangements are generally coordinated locally by a funeral service provider in the destination country, in cooperation with the Swiss funeral director responsible for the repatriation.

Families generally have two main options for the final disposition of the deceased:

- Burial: The coffin containing the deceased is placed in a grave at a cemetery. Burial remains a traditional form of funeral practice in many cultures and religions.
- Cremation: The deceased is cremated and the ashes are placed in a funeral urn and returned to the family. Depending on the family’s wishes and local regulations, cremation may take place before or after repatriation.
In some circumstances, families may choose to repatriate the ashes rather than the deceased, which can significantly simplify certain administrative procedures and reduce transportation costs.

## How Can You Reduce the Cost of Body Repatriation?

While the cost of repatriating a deceased person can be significant, there are several ways to plan ahead and potentially reduce the expenses involved. Here are some options to consider:

- Arrange funeral planning during your lifetime: This is one of the most effective ways to anticipate funeral and repatriation costs. It allows you to plan the arrangements in advance and determine how they will be funded.
